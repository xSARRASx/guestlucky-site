The journey

From Bangkok to GuestLucky, 10 years of adventure.

The real milestones - not a pitch-deck roadmap.

2009 · Bangkok

Subletting an apartment to pay rent

It all starts in Thailand. For personal reasons, I have to sublet my apartment. I discover short-term rental. Back then, I didn't see the business model yet - but I felt there was money to be made.

2014 · Bangkok

Launching the first concierge

After a brutal bankruptcy in France that wiped me out, no more stock business, no more bank loans. Concierge service is my new bet. I see empty properties everywhere, no one managing them seriously. I dive in.

2016 · 30 properties

Check-ins at 2 AM on a scooter

30 properties, 100% physical check-ins. Premium service. But Thai airports run all night. I'm out at 2am, 3am, 4am on my scooter. Huge selling point for owners, but unmanageable.

2017 · The wake-up

I miss a check-in. I overslept.

One night I'm scrolling my Airbnb bookings on my phone, mix up the day, fall asleep. Guest stranded at the door. That's the trigger. I need software. Two sleepless nights in my bed in Bangkok, I code GuestLucky v1.

2018

Airbnb locks the APIs. GuestLucky dies overnight.

Airbnb locks down the API. Booking follows. To access reservations you need a token. The token is given only to a handful of huge US-based players. I fill the form 10 times - never hear back. GuestLucky v1 dies.

2019 · France

Back in France, launching Le Sous Loueur

I move back, relaunch a concierge company that grows to 100 properties. I rebuild GuestLucky as an ERP this time - operational, not Channel Manager. My students in coaching beg me to open it up.

2024

First commercial version (ERP only)

We release the first commercial version, ERP only. It works, but we plateau. People want both - operational AND Channel Manager.

2026

GuestLucky all-in-one (CM + ERP + AI)

Early 2026, we ship the full version: Channel Manager + ERP + guest AI + Lucky Copilot. Today: +10,000 properties in France and abroad.

10 years in Airbnb

Field, field, and more field.

I started Airbnb in 2016, before it became an industry. I managed my own properties, then others' on mandate, then through sublease. I tried Beds24, Smoobu, SuperHôte, Hospitable, Hostaway, Guesty. All of them.

Each had one thing they did well and three things that frustrated me daily. A Channel Manager that crashed, an AI that talked like a robot, hidden 15% fees, onboardings that lasted 3 months. Every time I thought: "this could be so much better."

Except in my head, it wasn't frustration. It was a spec sheet.

8 years training pros

I've trained thousands of managers. I know what hurts.

In parallel, I launched Le Sous Loueur, a training organization dedicated to short-term rentals. For 8 years, I've trained pro property managers, sub-leasers, real-estate investors. Thousands have gone through my seminars, coaching, and masterminds.

When you train 300 people for 8 years, you hear the same problems over and over. Unsynced listings. Cleaners who don't know where to go. Guests waiting for a reply at 11pm. Deposits that take 6 months to return. Multi-tab Excels that explode at 15 properties.

I had the exhaustive list of industry pain points. Just needed the tool to fix them.

Software engineer

The engineer in me finally said: "I'll build it."

Few people know this: I'm a software engineer by training. Before Airbnb, I built enterprise software. So after 10 years of struggling with existing tools, when I realized I had both skill sets — field experience AND dev skills — I decided to get to work.

That's how GuestLucky was born. Not in an incubator. No VC raise. Without asking "users" what they wanted. I am the user. I know exactly what's needed, because I've been living the problem for 10 years.

Today the tool runs +10,000 properties, and the roadmap is decided by what I see in the field — not by a product manager who's never welcomed a guest.

Little side note

And what about the laundry bags on the scooter?

For anyone who thinks concierge work is glamorous: here's the real "laundry transport setup" I used for 3 years in Bangkok - a motorbike with one bag in front, two behind, bungee-corded together. I have the photos. Maybe I'll release them someday.

My first "laundry room"? A converted apartment with washing machines running all night. I slept inside, in a separate room. Definitely not the kind of story you tell on a startup pitch deck.

But it's exactly why, today, when a GuestLucky user tells me "I can't handle my laundry anymore", I know exactly what they mean. The laundry feature in GuestLucky was designed with those memories in mind - not from a Figma on a brand-new MacBook.

Sébastien's vision

The future of concierge work, from someone who's been there

Robots, AI, digital concierges: what's coming in the next 3-5 years, seen by someone who's been in the field for 10 years.

1-2 yrs

Robots arrive

Vacuum robots, mower robots already exist. But also foldable wall-mounted robots to clean bathrooms. China builds them, Europe distributes them. Months away, not years.

2-3 yrs

Fully digital concierges

For small properties (studios, T2s in cities), concierges will be fully digital. No human touches a key. Auto check-in, 24/7 guest AI, cleaning piloted remotely, laundry shuttled by autonomous vehicle.

3-5 yrs

The concierge profession shifts

The new job: help owners install their own system. You install, configure, train, hand them the software. No more "I take 25% commission". A real tech expertise that sells.

5+ yrs

Humans keep value on premium villas

Beautiful villas, top-tier travelers: they'll always want human contact. Digital for volume, premium for relationship. Both models will coexist.

Always

Operations stay essential

You can run without a Channel Manager (if Airbnb-only). The ERP, however, is non-negotiable. Who cleans when, what broke, reporting, checklists. No AI will fully replace that. You need software. Always.

2026

Our job: prep you for all this

GuestLucky already covers it. Channel Manager + ERP + guest AI + laundry/cleaning automations. You jump 5 years ahead by signing now, without paying 5 years up front.
